# CEFR alignment (A2 to B1)

All descriptor wording below is copied from the CEFR Companion Volume (Council of Europe, 2020) or the
CEFR global scale page, both opened on 2026-10-27 (research/sources.md S1, S2). Page numbers are the
Companion Volume's printed page numbers. The Companion Volume's tables split some levels into a lower and
an upper ("plus") band. Band labels were read from the rendered table layout; where a row had no visible
split, the plain level is given. The one B1+ label (Overall oral interaction) follows the paragraph order
in the extracted text and was not checked visually.

**What this alignment claims:** each objective is *informed by* these descriptors, and each task is designed
to give practice in the situations they describe. **What it does not claim:** that completing the game shows
a learner is at A2 or B1. No learner has played the game yet, and the in-game can-do statements are
self-assessment, not a test.

## Global scale (S1)

- **A2:** "Can understand sentences and frequently used expressions related to areas of most immediate relevance (e.g. very basic personal and family information, shopping, local geography, employment). Can communicate in simple and routine tasks requiring a simple and direct exchange of information on familiar and routine matters. Can describe in simple terms aspects of his/her background, immediate environment and matters in areas of immediate need."
- **B1:** "Can understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar matters regularly encountered in work, school, leisure, etc. Can deal with most situations likely to arise whilst travelling in an area where the language is spoken. Can produce simple connected text on topics which are familiar or of personal interest. Can describe experiences and events, dreams, hopes & ambitions and briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans."

## Level span

- The **floor** is A2: every task can be completed with A2 transaction language ("Could I have ...", "A return to ...").
- The **reach** is B1: task 4 needs a complaint and a negotiated solution; the effective options in tasks 1 and 3
  include checking and confirming information, and the repair strategies include B1 "clarify or elaborate".
- Odd options target pragmatic competence (tone, register, blame) rather than grammar, because a learner moving from A2 to B1 is
  expected to cope with "less routine situations" (p. 72), where tone matters.
