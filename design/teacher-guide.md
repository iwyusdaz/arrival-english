# Teacher guide

**Arrival** is a task-based English game for learners at about CEFR A2 working towards B1. Learners use English to
get four things done in a fictional city. It runs in a browser (desktop or phone) with no login, and everything
is saved only on the learner's device.

## Before class (10 min, teacher)
- Play task 1 yourself in text mode to see the three stages (Before / Task / After).
- Decide on audio: with headphones, learners can use the browser voice; without, everything works as text.
- Speech input is off by default. If you want learners to try it, tell them it is optional. In some browsers
  (for example Chrome) the voice is sent to the browser company's speech service to turn it into text. The game
  does not record or store audio.

## Suggested lesson (50-60 min)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Warm-up: "You've just arrived in a new city. What four things do you need to do first?" Elicit tickets, food, a pharmacy, accommodation. |
| 5 min | Learners complete the can-do self-assessment on the Start page. |
| 25-30 min | Learners play tasks 1-4 in pairs (one reads, one decides) or alone. Encourage them to choose the odd or wrong answer at least once, to see the reaction, and to use the "Need help?" buttons. |
| 10 min | Debrief (below). |
| 5 min | Listening check, or set it for homework with the phrasebook review. |

## Debrief questions
1. **Tone:** which answer made the staff member fold their arms? What was wrong with it, if the grammar was correct? (Task 1 "Your machine is broken. Fix it.", task 4 "That's not my problem.")
2. **Repair:** which help phrases did you use? Show your post-task table. What else can you say when you don't understand? (*What does ... mean? So ..., is that right?*)
3. **Safety language:** why must you say "She's allergic to nuts" *before* you choose a dish? What would you do with a real health problem? (Ask a pharmacist or doctor.)
4. **Form focus:** *since yesterday* vs *for two days*; *every three hours*; *I booked it last week*; *Which platform does it leave from?*
5. **Variety:** British *return / single, take away*; US *round-trip / one-way, to go*.

## Language by task
| Task | Key chunks (pre-taught) |
|---|---|
| 1 Station | Excuse me, can you help me? · My card doesn't work in the machine. · A return to ..., please. · Can I pay by card? · Which platform does it leave from? · Go straight on, then turn left. |
| 2 Café | Could I have ..., please? · And for my friend, ... · She's allergic to nuts. · Does it have any nuts in it? · I'll have the ... instead. · That's all, thanks. |
| 3 Pharmacy | I've got a sore throat. · since yesterday / for two days · Can you recommend something for ...? · I'm not taking any other medicine. · How often should I take it? · Should I see a doctor? |
| 4 Hostel | I have a booking under the name ... · I booked it online last week. · Here's my confirmation email. · Is there another room available? · Would it be possible to ...? · That works for me. |

## Follow-up speaking task (transfer, 10 min)
Role cards for pairs, with no screen: (a) a bus driver can't change a large note, (b) a restaurant has no
vegetarian option, (c) a hotel gives you a room with a broken heater. Learners must use one repair phrase and
one "Would it be possible to ...?".

## Learner records
Menu → My progress shows the local learning record (xAPI-shaped JSON). Learners can download it and send it to
you if you ask. "Clear my data" removes it. Nothing is sent anywhere automatically.

## Limits to tell learners
- The voices are the browser's own and can sound robotic.
- The speech check only listens for a key phrase. It is approximate and does not score pronunciation.
- The pharmacy task is language practice only, not medical advice.
- The can-do page is self-assessment, not a test or a CEFR level.
