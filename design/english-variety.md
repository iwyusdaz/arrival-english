# English variety and spelling standard

**Decision:** British English spelling and punctuation conventions (as in Oxford and Cambridge learner
dictionaries), with variety-neutral vocabulary where a neutral option exists.

| Area | Convention used | Examples in content |
|---|---|---|
| Spelling | British | *Harbour* City, Café, Pharmacy and Hostel; US forms such as *color, center, favorite* do not appear (a unit test spot-checks this) |
| Grammar forms | British-leaning, also understood in US English | *I've got a sore throat*; *have you had*; *sorting it out* |
| Titles | No full stop | *Mr Okafor* |
| Times | 12-hour with *am / pm* in speech and signs; 24-hour on screens | "Check-in from 2 pm"; departures board 10:15 |
| Money | Spoken numbers, no currency symbol | "That's eleven forty" |
| Vocabulary | Neutral where possible; British where needed | *return / single* (ticket), *take away*, *platform*; no *gas, apartment, check (bill)* |
| Voice | `en-GB` requested for browser speech; the actual voice depends on the device | |

**Why British spelling:** the vocabulary lists used for the level check (Cambridge A2 Key / B1 Preliminary)
are British-English based and mark US alternatives, so one consistent variety keeps the check meaningful.
Harbour City is fictional and not tied to any country. Accents depend on the learner's browser voices, so
the game does not claim a regional accent.

**Note for teachers:** US equivalents (one-way / round-trip ticket, to go) are worth mentioning in the
debrief (teacher-guide.md).
