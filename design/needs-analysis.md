# Needs analysis

## Problem

Learners at A2 often know words and grammar but freeze in real service encounters: a ticket machine that
rejects a card, a sold-out dish, a pharmacist's follow-up questions, a booking that "isn't there". These moments
need transactional language, repair strategies ("Sorry, could you repeat that?") and a sense of tone. Textbook
dialogues rarely let learners try a blunt or wrong answer and see what happens.

## Who it is for

Adult and older-teen learners at about CEFR A2 working towards B1: newcomers, international students and
travellers. It works as self-study (30-40 minutes) or before a speaking lesson (see teacher-guide.md).

## Constraints

- No API keys and no server: all dialogue is authored and runs in the browser.
- Desktop first, phones supported (portrait layout for dialogue).
- Low anxiety: no timers, help always visible, no grades (S6 in research/sources.md).
- Must work without sound and without a microphone (deaf and hard-of-hearing learners, shared spaces, privacy).

## What the game must do (from the plan)

1. Four real tasks, each a pre-task / task / post-task cycle.
2. Branching dialogue with effective, pragmatically odd and common-error options, each with feedback and an NPC reaction.
3. Three response modes: choose, build from chunk tiles, and optional speech (off by default).
4. Repair always available; captions always shown; speech rate 0.75-1.25x; transcripts after listening.
5. A phrasebook with spaced review, a listening check, a can-do self-assessment.
6. Text mode with the same tasks and decisions.

## What it does not try to do

Open conversation, grammar teaching, pronunciation scoring, levels above B1, or medical advice.
