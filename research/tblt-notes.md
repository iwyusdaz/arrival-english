# TBLT and evidence notes

## Task-based language teaching (design framework)

The game follows the plan's pre-task / task / post-task cycle, a common way to structure task-based
language teaching. The usual reference is Ellis (2003), *Task-based Language Learning and Teaching*
(OUP). Its bibliographic details were verified (sources S7), but the book was not opened for this
build. Nothing here quotes it, and the cycle below is described in this project's own words.

| Stage | In Arrival | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-task | A short model exchange in a *different* place with different details, 5-6 key chunks in bold, a quick matching activity, readable signage | Learners notice useful language before they need it, without being handed the answers |
| Task | A real goal with a twist (card rejected, dish sold out, follow-up questions, missing booking). The NPC reacts to tone and accuracy. Repair is always available. | Meaning first. The outcome (ticket, safe lunch, instructions, bed) is the measure of success |
| Post-task | Results table, chunks used well, recasts of odd or incorrect choices with explanations, new phrasebook entries, option to retry | Focus on form after the meaning-focused task; recasts give the better form without blocking progress |

## What counts as "a task" here

Each scene has a non-linguistic outcome (a ticket, a nut-free meal, dosage understood, a bed for the night)
that the learner reaches through language. Odd and incorrect choices still lead to an outcome, often by a
longer route (the NPC asks again, or rephrases). This keeps the experience low-stakes, in line with S6
(psychological safety lowers anxiety).

## Evidence, read honestly

- S4 and S5 are about **immersive VR**. Arrival is desktop and mobile 3D, so these studies are at most
  indirect support for context-rich practice, not evidence that this game works.
- S6 supports the low-anxiety choices (no timers, repair always available, limited clutter) and warns
  about cognitive overload. That is why the scene has no ambient crowd and no decorative motion during tasks.
- No learning gains are claimed. No learners have used the game yet (see evaluation/METHODS.md).
