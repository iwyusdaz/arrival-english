# Language level check

Generated by `node scripts/readability-check.mjs` (full output: `evaluation/results/readability.json`).

## Method

- **Readability:** Flesch Reading Ease (FRE) and Flesch-Kincaid grade, per task, for NPC lines,
  learner options and feedback. These formulas were built for native-speaker prose. They are not
  CEFR-calibrated and only signal sentence and word length.
- **Vocabulary:** every word in NPC lines and learner options is looked up in the Cambridge English
  A2 Key and B1 Preliminary vocabulary lists (UCLES/CUPA, August 2025; see sources S12), plus the A2
  Appendix 1 number words. Lemmatisation is crude (regular inflections, common irregulars, contractions).
  Names of fictional people and places, spelled-out letters and interjections are excluded.

## Interpretation and actions

- Sentences are short (5-6 words in dialogue). Feedback text is the hardest layer (FRE about 79-93),
  which is acceptable because it is read, not heard, and can be reread at any pace.
- Most off-list words are the task's own content words. They cannot be avoided without changing the
  task (an allergy task needs "allergic"; a pharmacy task needs "pharmacist"). Each one is supported:
  - pre-taught as a key chunk in the pre-task ("She's allergic to nuts", "Can you recommend something for ...?"),
  - explained in the dialogue when the learner uses a repair button (the "Could you spell that?" and
    "What does ... mean?" turns gloss *off-peak, swallowing, lozenges, dormitory*), or
  - printed on readable signage (menu notes "contains walnuts", the product box).
- "frustrating" and "useless" appear once each, in an NPC line and in a pragmatically odd option. They were kept
  because they model the emotional register of a complaint. "stairs" is on neither list as a headword
  (the lists may cover it under another entry); it is kept as a basic directions word.
- Limits: list membership is not a full measure of difficulty (collocations, idioms, speed and accent
  matter too). An expert-style ESL review is part of the orchestrator's reviews.
